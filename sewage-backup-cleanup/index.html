Drain Backed Up With Raw Sewage?

Water that comes up through a floor drain, toilet or tub and smells like sewage is the kind of loss most homeowners have never handled before. Callers describe a basement drain that backed up and filled the floor with raw sewage. It is not something to mop up yourself.

In the trade this is Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, meaning it can carry bacteria and other contaminants. The cleanup is a protected, controlled process.

While You Wait

Keep people out

Close doors to the area and keep children and pets away.

Do not use water

Avoid flushing toilets or running water until the line is cleared.

Shut off power if wet

If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off power from a dry spot.

Do not use a vacuum

A household or shop vacuum can spread contamination. Leave extraction to the crew.

How Sewage Cleanup Works

The goal is to remove the contamination and what it soaked into, then clean what remains.

Containment

We set up barriers where needed and wear protective gear so nothing is tracked through the house.

Removal

Liquid is extracted. Carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that absorbed sewage are usually removed and disposed of properly.

Cleaning and drying

Remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated with suitable products, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Insurance and Sewage Backup

DFS notes that policies may cover back-up and sewage damage through an endorsement. Check your policy and ask your agent. Document the loss before cleanup and keep receipts for emergency work.

Items That Usually Cannot Be Kept After Sewage Contact

  • Carpet, pad and upholstered furniture.
  • Mattresses, pillows and soft toys.
  • Cardboard boxes and stored paper.
  • Wet drywall, insulation and particleboard.

Why Professional Gear Matters

Sewage is a contamination problem, not just a water problem.

Protective equipment

Crews wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators suited to the job.

Cleaning products

Surfaces are cleaned and treated with products chosen for contamination, then dried.

Disposal

Contaminated materials are bagged and handled as waste rather than left at the curb.

How to Reduce the Risk of Another Backup

A plumber can camera the line to find roots, grease or a collapse. Avoid flushing wipes and pouring grease down drains. A backwater valve may be an option where a main line serves several homes.

If the home is on a septic system, a septic contractor can check the tank and the drain field.

Septic Systems and Sewer Lines in Suffolk County

Many Suffolk County homes use septic systems rather than public sewers, while others connect to sewer lines. Ask whether your address is sewered or on a septic system, because the source of a backup differs.

A plumber clears the blockage or a septic contractor services the system. We handle the cleanup. Ask your building department about permits for any repairs.
