Drywall soaks up water and fails fast. Stains, soft spots and holes mean moisture is in the wall. We find the source, dry the cavity and repair the wall.

What Water Does to Drywall

Drywall is gypsum between paper. When it gets wet, the paper holds moisture and the gypsum softens. A wall that stays wet can crumble, grow mold on the paper and swell at the baseboard.

Callers describe a hole forming in a kitchen wall, a damp corner in the shower area and water damage from the siding of the house. Each has a different source.

Where Wall Water Comes From

Tracing the source is half the repair.

Plumbing in the wall

A pinhole or loose fitting leaks inside the cavity. The stain appears on the surface after the paper is soaked.

Exterior leaks

Siding, windows and flashing can let rain in behind the wall. See storm water damage cleanup.

Bathrooms

Shower corners and tub surrounds can leak behind tile. The leak often shows at the corner of the wall and ceiling.

Drywall Repair Process

Locate

Moisture meters and a thermal camera find the wet area.

Open

Wet drywall and insulation are cut out.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the framing.

Rebuild

New drywall is hung, taped, textured and painted.

Drywall and Mold

Wet drywall that stays wet for days can grow mold on the paper facing. If you see dark spots, read mold remediation. New drywall goes up only after the framing is dry.

Plaster, Paint and Texture Matching

Getting the finished wall to match is a craft step.

Plaster and older walls

Older plaster walls hold water differently from modern drywall. Repairs are patched in layers and need time to cure between coats.

Texture

Orange peel, knockdown and smooth finishes each need the matching technique. A patch without texture shows under light.

Paint

New drywall is primed before paint. Painting the whole wall to the nearest corner avoids a visible patch edge.

Signs a Wall Needs to Be Opened

  • The drywall is soft, bulging or crumbling to the touch.
  • Moisture readings are high even after drying.
  • Paint keeps bubbling in the same place.
  • A smell of mildew comes from the wall.

Questions to Ask Before Drywall Goes Back Up

Closing a wall too early traps moisture.

Is the framing dry?

Meter readings on studs and plates should be at normal levels.

Is the source fixed?

A plumber, roofer or siding contractor should confirm the leak is stopped.

Is insulation replaced?

Wet insulation that was removed should be replaced before closing the wall.

When a Wall Can Be Dried Without Opening It

Some walls can be dried through small holes at the baseboard. Warm, dry air is pushed through the cavity and moisture comes out through the same holes. This works when the drywall is still firm and the insulation is not saturated.

Soft, stained or bulging drywall is cut out, because it will not regain strength after drying.

What to Do and What Not to Do With Wet Drywall

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Turn off power to outlets in the wet wall and shut the water if a pipe is the source.
  • Do not paint or caulk over a damp wall, since that traps moisture in the cavity.
  • Do not poke holes in a bulging wall unless water is pooling behind it. Release it into a bucket.
  • Wet insulation and drywall follow ANSI/IICRC S500 categories, and board that touched contaminated water comes out.

Older Walls in Brentwood Homes

Houses built decades ago may have plaster or older drywall that reacts differently to water. Older homes also have older pipes and flashing that fail with age.

If studs or wiring are touched, ask your building department whether a permit applies.
