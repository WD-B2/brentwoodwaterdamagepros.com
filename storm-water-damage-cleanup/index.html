After the Storm: Water in the House

Wind-driven rain, a damaged roof, a failed window seal or a basement that fills during a downpour can all bring storm water indoors. Callers describe leaks that start suddenly during heavy rain and a roof that has sprung a leak overnight.

Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings and keep away from wet electrical. Call as soon as it is safe and tell us where the water entered.

Safe First Steps

Protect people

Stay clear of ceilings that sag or fixtures that drip near wiring.

Cover openings

If a roof or window is open, a temporary tarp reduces more water entering. Do this only if it is safe.

Document

Photograph damage inside and out before repairs. DFS recommends documenting losses before cleaning up.

Call your insurer

Ask about coverage and keep receipts for emergency repairs.

Storm Water Entry Points

Where the water came in decides who fixes the cause.

Roof and attic

Missing shingles or flashing failure let water into the attic and through ceilings. A roofer fixes the roof. We handle the interior drying and repair. See ceiling water damage repair.

Windows and doors

Wind-driven rain pushes past seals. Water runs down walls and into flooring near the opening.

Basement and foundation

Heavy rain can raise groundwater or overwhelm drains. See basement water damage cleanup for below-grade water.

Insurance After Storm Damage

Wind and rain damage are often covered, while flood is typically excluded from standard homeowners policies according to NY DFS. Check your policy and consider the FEMA flood map for your address.

Documenting Storm Damage for a Claim

Good records speed up the conversation with your insurer.

Take wide and close photos

Photograph each damaged room from a doorway and then close up on the damage. Include the outside of the house where water entered.

Keep temporary repair receipts

Tarps, plywood and emergency services are usually reimbursable if the policy covers the loss. Save every receipt.

Wait on permanent repairs

DFS advises that permanent repairs should wait until the insurer has inspected the loss.

Why Storm Losses Come in Waves

Storms often cause more than one problem at once, such as a roof leak plus basement seepage plus wet insulation. Crews triage by safety first, then by what is still taking on water.

If power is out, tell us. We plan lighting and equipment power around it, and we advise on what not to run until an electrician has looked.

What to Do and What Not to Do After Storm Water Enters

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Stay clear of downed lines and wet outlets, and do not wade through water that touches the panel.
  • Treat water that came from outside as contaminated under ANSI/IICRC S500. Wear boots and gloves.
  • Do not use food that touched floodwater, and discard wet porous items that cannot be cleaned.
  • Tarp the roof opening if you can do it safely from the ground, and photograph the damage.
