Carpet Water Damage in Brentwood, NY

Wet carpet can sometimes be dried and saved, but the pad underneath often cannot. We check the water source and the pad and tell you plainly what stays and what goes.

Can Wet Carpet Be Saved?

It depends on three things: how clean the water was, how long the carpet stayed wet and how thick the pad is. Carpet wet from a clean supply line and caught within a day or two can often be dried in place. Carpet exposed to sewage or floodwater is normally removed.

Callers often say they pulled up a corner and found mold on the back. That is a sign the carpet stayed wet too long.

What Decides Save or Replace

We read moisture and look at the material before making that call.

Water category

Clean water gives you the best chance. Gray water or sewage means carpet and pad are discarded under ANSI/IICRC S500 guidance.

Time wet

Within 24 to 48 hours is the rough window for good results. Longer than that raises odor and mold risk.

Pad and subfloor

Pad holds water like a sponge. It is often cheaper to replace than to dry. The subfloor underneath still has to be dried.

Carpet Restoration Process

Extract

Wand extraction removes as much water as possible.

Inspect

We check the pad, tack strip and subfloor with meters.

Dry or remove

Carpet is lifted and dried, or removed when it cannot be saved.

Reinstall

Clean carpet is stretched back or new carpet and pad installed after drying.

Carpet, Mold and Odor

A damp carpet that never fully dried can smell musty and grow mold on the underside. If you see growth, read mold remediation. Dry the area quickly and fix the source first.

What Happens to the Pad and Tack Strip

The carpet gets the attention, but the layers under it decide the outcome.

Pad

Foam or fiber pad soaks up water and releases it slowly. Even when carpet is saved, the pad is usually replaced because it holds moisture and is inexpensive to swap.

Tack strip

The wooden strips at the edges hold the carpet in place. They swell, can stain the carpet and sometimes rot if left wet.

Subfloor

Plywood or concrete beneath the pad has to reach dry readings before new pad goes down. Skipping this step is how odors return.

Signs Carpet Was Dried Too Late

  • A musty smell that returns on humid days.
  • Dark staining along the edges where it meets the wall.
  • Rippling that does not flatten after stretching.
  • Mold on the back when a corner is lifted.

What to Do While You Wait

Keep foot traffic off wet carpet because walking pushes water deeper. Lift furniture legs onto foil or plastic so stain does not transfer. Open windows if the air outside is dry and the weather allows.

Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet. Leave extraction to equipment built for it.

Carpeted Basements and Bedrooms

Many Brentwood homes have carpet in finished basements and bedrooms. Basement carpet is more likely to meet groundwater or drain backups, which changes the water category.

If walls or subfloors are rebuilt, ask your building department about permits.
