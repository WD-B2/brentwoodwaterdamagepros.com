Appliance Leak Emergency: Stop It, Then Call

Close the valve behind the appliance if you can reach it. For a washer, that is the pair of hot and cold hose valves. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and floor are dry.

Washer hoses fail under pressure. A valve that breaks can run for an hour or more before anyone notices, soaking flooring and the room below.

Common Appliance Leak Sources

Each appliance leaks in a different way, and the water goes to a different place.

Washing machines

Supply hoses, drain hoses and the valve itself can fail. Water runs under flooring and into the ceiling or room below. Hardwood and laminate close to a laundry room are the most affected.

Dishwashers

A failed door seal, supply line or drain connection wets the cabinet base and the floor near the sink. Kitchen cabinet toe kicks absorb it first. See kitchen water damage.

Refrigerators and ice makers

A cracked ice maker line leaks slowly behind or under the unit. The floor can stay wet for weeks without anyone knowing.

Hardwood and Laminate After a Washer Leak

A washer valve that breaks and runs unnoticed for an hour or more is a common story, and hardwood is the main casualty. Quick drying with controlled airflow can save boards, but cupping can appear a day or two after the leak.

See hardwood floor water damage repair if boards have started to lift or crown.

Preventing the Next Appliance Leak

Most hose and valve failures give some warning if you look.

Inspect supply hoses

Look for bulges, cracks and rust at the fittings on a washer or dishwasher. Rubber hoses age, and braided steel hoses last longer but still need checking.

Know where the shutoff is

Every household member should know where the appliance valves and the main shutoff are. Seconds matter when a hose lets go.

Pull the unit out once a year

A quick look behind a refrigerator or washer catches slow drips before they rot the subfloor.

What to Tell Us When You Call

Tell us which appliance failed, which floor it sits on and whether the room below is finished. Say how long the water ran, if you know. A hose that failed overnight is a different job from a drip that ran for weeks.

If a ceiling below has stains, mention that too, because water often travels down through the floor before it shows.

What to Do and What Not to Do After an Appliance Leak

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Close the appliance supply valve and unplug the unit only if the cord and outlet are dry.
  • Do not pull a refrigerator or washer across a wet floor, since the wheels can wick water into cabinets.
  • Lift rugs and box contents off the wet floor and note which items were affected.
  • Treat the water as clean only when it came from a supply line. A drain hose leak is gray water under ANSI/IICRC S500 and needs more care.

Laundry Rooms in Brentwood Houses

Many Long Island homes place the laundry in a basement or first-floor utility room. Water from a failed hose follows the floor toward the lowest point and often drips into the area below.

Appliance replacement may need a plumber. If cabinets or walls are rebuilt, ask your building department whether a permit applies.
