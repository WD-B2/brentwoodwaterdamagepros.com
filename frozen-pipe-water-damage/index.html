When a Frozen Pipe Starts Leaking

Ice expands inside a pipe and can split it along the side. The leak often shows up only after the temperature rises, when the ice melts and water starts moving. A sudden drip, a wet ceiling or a drop in pressure the morning after a freeze are typical signs.

Close the main shutoff right away. Then call the 24/7 line and tell us which wall, ceiling or floor is wet.

If a Pipe Is Frozen but Not Yet Leaking

Do not wait for a split. These steps reduce the chance of one.

  • Open the faucet fed by the frozen line so melting water has somewhere to go.
  • Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, never an open flame or torch.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to let room air reach the pipes.
  • Shut the main water valve if you see any wet spot forming.
  • Call a plumber for pipes you cannot reach or thaw safely.

Where Freeze Damage Tends to Show Up

Pipes in unheated or poorly insulated spaces are the usual victims. The location tells us how far the water has traveled.

Exterior walls

A split inside an exterior wall wets the insulation and drywall from the inside. Paint may bubble or stain long before water drips. We use moisture meters and a thermal camera to find the wet area.

Garages, attics and crawl spaces

Water from a split line above a ceiling or in an unheated space can soak insulation and drip into finished rooms. In a crawl space it can saturate the framing, which is covered in crawl space water damage.

Sprinkler and outdoor lines

A frozen outdoor spigot connection can split inside the wall. The leak runs into the house even though the pipe is outside.

What We Do After a Freeze Break

Assess

We trace the water path and check moisture in walls, ceilings and floors.

Extract

Standing water is removed and saturated insulation or drywall is cut out.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry standards.

Restore

Drywall, trim and paint are repaired once the structure is dry.

Insurance and Freeze Claims

Many homeowners policies treat sudden freeze breaks as covered losses, but conditions apply, such as keeping the home heated. Check your policy or ask your agent. New York DFS advises documenting the loss and filing promptly.

What to Do and What Not to Do With Frozen Pipes

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Open the faucet and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer on a low setting, starting near the tap.
  • Do not use a torch or open flame, which has started house fires in wall cavities.
  • Shut the main valve at the first sign of a split pipe or a wet ceiling.
  • Keep cabinet doors open on cold nights so room air reaches pipes on exterior walls.
