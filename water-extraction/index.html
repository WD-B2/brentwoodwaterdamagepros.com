If Water Is Standing in Your Home Right Now

Water that sits on a floor wicks upward into drywall, baseboards and cabinet bases. Carpet padding holds it like a sponge. The first few hours decide how much of that material can be dried in place and how much has to be cut out.

Our 24/7 line is staffed day and night, weekends and holidays. Tell us where the water came from, how deep it is and whether the power is still on. We dispatch based on that and explain the plan before work starts. Same-day response is available when crews are free.

While You Wait for the Crew

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff if you can reach it without wading through water near outlets. Stopping the flow comes before everything else.

Cut power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, turn off the breaker from a dry spot. If you cannot reach it dry, stay out and call an electrician.

Lift what you can

Move small furniture, rugs and boxes onto dry surfaces. Foil or plastic under furniture legs keeps stain transfer down on wet carpet.

Photograph first

Take photos and short video of the water line and affected items before anything is moved. Insurers ask for this, and New York DFS tells homeowners to document losses before cleaning up.

How Extraction Works

Extraction is the removal of bulk liquid water. It comes before drying because evaporation alone is far too slow when there is an inch of water on the floor.

Equipment on the job

Crews use portable extraction units for small rooms and truck-mounted units for large or deep losses. A wand tool pulls water out of carpet and pad. On hard floors a squeegee head feeds the same machines.

Reading the water

Water is sorted by how contaminated it is, using the categories in the ANSI/IICRC S500 industry standard. Clean supply water is Category 1. Water from a washer or dishwasher drain is Category 2. Sewage and floodwater are Category 3 and change the whole plan, including what must be thrown away.

Checking what remains

After the standing water is gone, moisture meters read wood, drywall and subfloor. A thermal camera shows cool, wet areas behind finished surfaces. The readings set the drying plan and are logged so you can see progress.

Water Extraction Is Not the Whole Job

Pulling out standing water is the visible part. Moisture that already moved into walls and subfloors keeps working after the floor looks dry, which is how musty smells and mold start. That is why extraction is paired with structural drying and, where growth has begun, mold remediation.

When the water came from a pipe

A supply line break usually sends clean water, so more materials can be saved. See burst pipe water damage for the shutoff and cleanup steps specific to that cause.

When the water came from outside

Rain, runoff and rising groundwater carry soil and bacteria. Flood damage cleanup covers the added precautions.

What to Do and What Not to Do Before Extraction Starts

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Stop the source and keep people and pets out of the standing water.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on water. A wet and dry shop vacuum is the only safe option for small spills.
  • Do not turn on ceiling fixtures that sit under a wet ceiling.
  • Lift valuables off the floor and photograph the waterline first.
