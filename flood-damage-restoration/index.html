Floodwater Is Not Clean Water

Water that enters from outside, from a storm drain or from rising groundwater is treated as contaminated. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard puts it in Category 3. That means porous materials such as carpet, pad and wet drywall are usually removed rather than dried.

Stay out of standing water if you can. If power is on in the area, do not wade in. Turn off the breaker from a dry spot or call an electrician.

Before the Crew Arrives

Stay safe

Keep children and pets out of the water. Floodwater can hide hazards and carry bacteria.

Protect important items

Move documents, electronics and photographs to a dry, high spot.

Document the water line

Photograph the high-water mark and damaged contents. DFS advises documenting losses before cleaning up.

Avoid DIY chemicals

Do not mix cleaners. Crews use products chosen for the contamination level.

How a Flood Cleanup Runs

The order matters. Contaminated material comes out first, and drying and treatment follow.

Pumping and extraction

Deep water is pumped out, followed by wand extraction on carpet and hard floors. Dirty water does not sit while the rest of the plan is made.

Removal of unsalvageable materials

Wet carpet, pad, insulation and drywall below the water line are cut out and bagged. Cutting a little above the water line catches the moisture that wicked upward.

Cleaning and treating

Remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated with products suited to the contamination. Wet framing and subfloor are then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

What Usually Stays and What Goes After a Flood

MaterialCommon outcomeReason
Carpet and pad.Removed.Porous, holds contamination.
Drywall below the water line.Cut out.Absorbs and wicks the water.
Solid wood framing.Cleaned and dried.Dense, can dry with treatment.
Concrete slab.Cleaned and dried.Non-porous surface, dries slowly.

Final decisions follow moisture readings and the contamination level on your property.

Flood Insurance Works Differently

New York DFS states that a typical homeowners policy does not cover flood damage. Coverage can come from a National Flood Insurance Program policy or a water back-up endorsement. Check your policy and ask your agent what applies.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address if you are unsure of your risk. Document the loss, save receipts and let your insurer inspect before making permanent repairs.

Flood Cleanup in Four Stages

Stabilize

We confirm hazards, shut off what can be shut off and plan protection.

Remove

Water and contaminated materials leave the property.

Dry and treat

Structure is cleaned and dried with logged moisture readings.

Rebuild

Drywall, flooring and finishes are replaced once everything tests dry.

Keep a Flood Damage Log

Good records help with claims and with cleanup decisions.

  • Date and time water first appeared and when it was removed.
  • Photos of the water line on walls, in each room and on furniture.
  • A list of damaged items with approximate age and replacement cost.
  • Receipts for any emergency work, equipment or hotel stays.

Flooding in Suffolk County Homes

Brentwood is in the Town of Islip, where many homes have basements that take water first. Heavy rain, a failed sump pump or a backed-up drain can all bring water in.

Restoration is not waterproofing. If water keeps coming back, you need a waterproofing contractor for the cause, and we will tell you so. Ask your building department about permits before structural rebuilds.
