Basement Water Emergency

Water in a basement can come from a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a sump pump failure, a backed-up drain or heavy rain. Callers tell us they have water coming into the basement and need someone to take a look.

Stay out if water is near outlets or the panel. Switch off power from a dry spot if you can. Then call the 24/7 line.

Where Basement Water Comes From

The source decides the water category and who fixes the cause.

Inside the house

Restoration Is Not Waterproofing

We restore damage. We do not waterproof foundations or install drainage. If a basement keeps taking on water, a waterproofing contractor should evaluate the cause, and we will tell you if that is what you need.

The First 72 Hours in a Wet Basement

Basement losses follow a rough timeline, and what happens early decides what can be saved.

Hours one to six

Water is stopped at the source and removed. Power to wet areas is shut off. Photos are taken and valuables moved up. This is the best window for saving drywall and framing.

Hours six to 24

Wet carpet and pad come out if they cannot be dried. Dehumidifiers and air movers start. Anything porous left wet begins to smell.

Hours 24 to 72

Mold starts to become possible on wet organic materials. Monitoring readings matters more than appearance during this period.

What to Move Out First

Prioritize these items if you can reach them safely.

  • Documents, photographs and electronics.
  • Boxes and cardboard on the floor, since cardboard wicks water upward.
  • Rugs and small furniture that can be carried out.
  • Anything stored against wet walls, where moisture hides behind it.

How Basement Drying Differs From Upstairs Rooms

Basements dry slower than upper floors. Concrete holds moisture, air moves less and humidity is already high in warm months. That is why basement jobs lean on dehumidifiers and why readings matter more than the calendar.

Finished basements also hide water behind paneling and under flooring, so we open small inspection points rather than guess from the surface.

What to Do and What Not to Do in a Flooded Basement

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Stay out of the basement while water covers outlets or the panel. Ask the utility to shut power off at the meter if the panel is wet.
  • Do not walk through standing water with a shop vacuum, and never run extension cords through it.
  • Do not open the basement door fast if water is deep behind it, because the weight can push the door.
  • Move to stairs and upper floors first, then photograph the waterline before anything changes.
  • Floodwater from outside is Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500, so wear boots and gloves and expect porous materials to come out.
