Commercial Water Loss Emergency

A broken sprinkler head, a failed roof drain, a burst supply line or a backed-up restroom stops work and puts inventory and records at risk. Water spreads across open floor plans faster than in a home and runs through ceiling tiles into spaces below.

Call from the site. Tell us the type of building, the source and which areas are wet. We plan the crew and equipment before arriving, and we explain scope and price before work starts.

What Differs From a Home Loss

Commercial jobs are about size, access and downtime.

Larger areas and equipment

Offices, retail floors and warehouses need more air movers and dehumidifiers, and sometimes truck-mounted extraction.

Working around operations

We schedule around business hours where we can and section off affected areas so unaffected spaces can stay open.

Documentation

Insurers ask for moisture logs, photographs and a clear scope. We provide the records you need for your claim.

Insurance and Business Interruption

Commercial policies vary widely. Some include business interruption coverage and some do not. Review your policy, ask your agent and keep receipts for emergency work. DFS guidance on documenting losses applies to businesses as well.

Protecting Contents, Inventory and Records

Business losses are often measured in inventory and files, not square feet.

Documents and electronics

Move paper records and equipment off wet floors first. Elevate items on plastic sheeting or onto shelving above the water line.

Stock and fixtures

Photograph stock before moving it, and keep a list. Your insurer will ask for an itemized loss.

Ceiling tiles and carpet tile

Wet ceiling tiles are usually replaced. Carpet tile can sometimes be lifted, dried and relaid, which shortens downtime.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire a Restoration Crew

  • What is the scope, and how will it be explained in writing?
  • How will moisture readings be recorded and shared?
  • Can work be staged around opening hours?
  • What documentation will you provide for the insurer?

Tenants, Landlords and Shared Walls

Water often travels from one unit to the next through shared walls and ceilings. Tell us which spaces belong to whom so we can document each area separately. Separate records make it easier for each party to deal with its own insurer.

If a neighbor's unit is the source, photograph that area where you can safely do so and share what you find with your building manager.

Standards We Work To in a Commercial Loss

Commercial drying follows the same ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage guide that homes use, with added attention to occupancy, tenants and records. We describe it as the benchmark we work to. It is not a claim of certification, and we do not substitute it for your insurer's own requirements.
