Why Kitchen Leaks Do So Much Damage

A kitchen packs plumbing, a dishwasher, a refrigerator line and cabinets into one small space. A drip under the sink soaks cabinet bases and spreads across the subfloor. Callers describe a burst pipe under the sink with water all across the floor, or a wall that has been wet from the siding outside.

The first signs are swollen cabinet edges, soft flooring near the sink and a musty smell when you open a cabinet.

Common Kitchen Sources

Each source leaves a different pattern.

Under-sink supply and drain lines

Fittings loosen, supply lines crack and drains leak. Water pools in the cabinet and runs under the flooring. See burst pipe water damage if it was a sudden break.

Dishwasher and refrigerator lines

Slow leaks run for weeks behind the unit. They can rot subfloor and feed mold. See appliance leak water damage.

Exterior wall leaks

Siding or window flashing failures let water into the wall behind kitchen cabinets. The stain shows on the interior wall long after the leak began.

Kitchen Restoration Process

Locate

We find the source and measure how far water spread.

Open

Toe kicks, cabinet bases and drywall are opened where needed.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity and floor.

Rebuild

Cabinets, flooring and drywall are repaired or replaced.

Hidden Water and Mold in Kitchens

A leak under a sink that runs for days can feed mold in the cabinet base and wall behind it. If you see dark spots, read mold remediation. The plumbing leak must be fixed first.

Countertops, Appliances and Plumbing During Kitchen Repair

Kitchen jobs touch more trades than most rooms.

Countertops

Cabinets often have to be detached to dry the base, which means countertops lift or are protected. Planning this early avoids damage.

Appliances

A dishwasher or refrigerator is pulled out to dry the floor beneath and behind it. A technician reconnects them after.

Plumbing

A plumber repairs the source. We time our drying so it does not block the plumber's access to the wall.

Signs a Kitchen Leak Has Been Ongoing

  • Warped or swollen cabinet base panels.
  • Dark staining at the back of the sink cabinet.
  • A floor that feels spongy near the dishwasher.
  • A persistent musty smell when cabinet doors are shut.

What to Tell Us When You Call

Tell us where you first noticed water, whether an appliance is nearby and whether the cabinet bases feel soft. Say if the wall behind the kitchen is an exterior wall, since siding and flashing leaks follow a different path.

Photos of cabinet interiors and the floor around the sink help us plan the crew and the tools before arriving.

What to Do and What Not to Do After a Kitchen Leak

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Shut the valve under the sink or behind the dishwasher and clear the cabinet base.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or disposal again until the leak source is found.
  • Pull items out of lower cabinets, since toe kicks and cabinet backs hold water out of sight.
  • Turn off power to wet outlets and avoid using the refrigerator cord if the floor is wet.

Drying Cabinets and Floors to a Standard

Kitchen losses involve cabinets, toe kicks and subfloor, which dry at different rates. The ANSI/IICRC S500 guide sets drying goals by material, so we compare each reading with a dry reference rather than a calendar number.

Kitchens in Older Long Island Homes

Brentwood has many mid-century and later houses with original kitchen plumbing and cabinets. Older fittings and supply lines are more likely to fail.

If cabinets or walls are rebuilt, ask your building department whether a permit applies.
