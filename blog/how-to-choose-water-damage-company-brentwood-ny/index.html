How to Choose a Water Damage Company in Brentwood, NY

Ask for a written scope, moisture readings, proof of insurance and, for mold work, a New York license. Be wary of pressure to sign on the spot or a single price with no detail. A company that explains the scope before work starts is a good sign.

By the Brentwood Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026

Why the Choice Matters

Water damage is urgent, which makes it easy to pick the first name you find. A rushed choice can lead to unclear pricing, missed moisture and disputes with your insurer. A few questions can sort out most problems.

This is a buyer's guide, not a ranking. It explains how to evaluate any company, including ours.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire

QuestionWhat a good answer sounds likeWarning sign
Is there a written scope?Yes, by room and material.Only a verbal number.
Do you take moisture readings?Yes, and they are logged and shared.Cannot show readings.
Are you insured?Provides proof on request.Evasive or vague.
Do you hold the needed license for mold?States the license and number for you to verify.No license for mold work.
How is pricing set?Explained by scope and days.Single number, no breakdown.
Can you work with my insurer?Provides photos and logs.Promises to decide coverage.

What to Verify

Do not rely on a company's own words alone.

Insurance

Ask for a certificate of insurance and check that it is current.

Mold licensing

The New York Department of Labor says mold assessors and remediators must be licensed for projects over ten square feet. Ask to see it and check it with the DOL.

Local presence

Check that the company has a real address and a phone that is answered. Our address is 739 Suffolk Avenue in Brentwood.

Red Flags

Pressure to sign immediately. Promises that the outcome is certain. No mention of moisture readings. A request for the full payment before work. Door-to-door sales after a storm. Offers to handle the insurance claim for you in ways that exclude you from the process.

What a Good Process Looks Like

A good job follows a clear sequence.

Assess

The crew walks the property, reads moisture and explains what is wet.

Scope and price

You see the scope and how the price is built before work begins.

Mitigate

Water comes out, wet materials that cannot be saved are removed and equipment starts. See water extraction.

Verify

Readings confirm drying. Records go to you.

Water Damage Is Not Waterproofing

Some callers hire a restoration company hoping to fix recurring basement water. Restoration repairs damage after water enters. A waterproofing contractor addresses how water gets in. A trustworthy company tells you which one you need.

What to Expect on the First Call

The first phone call tells you a lot.

Do they answer?

Emergency lines should be answered by a person, not an endless menu.

Do they ask good questions?

Expect questions about the source, the rooms, the water type and whether power is on near the water.

Do they explain next steps?

You should know what happens next and what to do while you wait.

Do they avoid promises?

Be wary of guarantees about arrival times or exact prices before anyone has seen the loss.

Estimating Without Seeing the Damage

Over the phone, a company can describe how pricing works and what factors apply. It cannot know the exact scope without readings. If a company gives a firm total price before seeing anything, ask what that price assumes.

A reasonable company explains the factors, then confirms scope on site.

A Short Checklist You Can Print

  • Written scope before work starts.
  • Proof of insurance.
  • Moisture readings shared with you.
  • License shown for mold work.
  • Clear explanation of how price is set.
  • No pressure to sign on the spot.

Questions About Equipment and Process

A company's equipment and process show how it will treat your home.

What equipment will you use?

Expect extraction units, air movers, dehumidifiers, moisture meters and a thermal camera.

How often will you check in?

A drying job needs monitoring visits. Ask how many and how readings are shared.

What happens when the job is done?

Ask for final readings and documentation.

Who will be at my home?

Ask whether the crew is employed by the company or subcontracted, and who is responsible on site.

Reviews, Ads and Directories

Online listings are a starting point. Look at how a company describes its process and whether the information is specific and consistent. A page that explains how moisture is measured says more than a page of stock phrases.

Be cautious of any listing that shows credentials you cannot verify. If a company states a license or certification, ask for the number and check it with the issuing body.

After You Choose

Confirm the plan in writing, ask who your point of contact is and keep the company's phone number handy. Take your own photos before work starts and again at each stage. Ask for a copy of the final moisture readings and an invoice that matches the scope.

If something does not match what you were told, say so right away and ask for a written explanation. Prompt communication fixes most misunderstandings before they become disputes.

Keep all records together for your insurer and for any future sale of the home.

Take Your Time Where You Can

Emergency mitigation should not wait, but larger decisions can. Once water is out and drying has started, you have time to read the scope, check licenses and compare rebuild quotes. A company that respects that pace is a good sign.

What We Would Want If It Were Our Home

Clear information, honest limits and records. We want to know what is wet, what the plan is and how the price is built. We want to be told when a problem belongs to another trade. And we want the readings in writing. Hold any company, including ours, to that standard.

Local Notes for Brentwood

Brentwood is in the Town of Islip. For permit questions on rebuild work, contact the Building Division. If a company says no permit is needed for plumbing or structural work, ask the Division to confirm.
