Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Brentwood, NY Homes

File the claim as soon as possible, document everything before cleanup, protect the home from further damage and keep every receipt. Wait for the insurer to inspect before permanent repairs. These steps follow guidance from the New York Department of Financial Services.

By the Brentwood Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026

Why Order Matters

A claim goes smoother when the evidence is gathered in the right order. Photos taken after cleanup show less than photos taken before. Permanent repairs made too early can leave the adjuster with nothing to inspect.

The steps below follow DFS advice for homeowners after a loss. They are general guidance and not legal advice.

The Four Phases of a Water Damage Claim

Stop and protect

Stop the water source if it is safe. Take emergency steps to prevent further damage.

Document

Photograph and video the damage and damaged items before cleanup.

Notify and file

Call your insurer or agent promptly and open the claim.

Repair in order

Make emergency repairs, wait for inspection, then do permanent repairs.

Step by Step

1. Make it safe

Shut off the water, switch off power to wet areas and keep people away from sagging ceilings. Safety comes before the claim.

2. Photograph before cleanup

Take wide shots of each room and close-ups of damage, the water line and the source. Short video with narration helps.

3. Make a list

Write down damaged items with approximate age and replacement cost. Keep any receipts you have.

4. Call the insurer

File as soon as possible after the loss. Ask for the claim number and the adjuster's contact information.

5. Take emergency steps

Remove standing water and wet materials that cannot be saved, and keep receipts. DFS says early work should be limited to repairs needed for health or safety or to prevent more damage.

6. Wait for the inspection

DFS says permanent repairs should not be made until the insurer has inspected the loss.

7. Keep the records

Moisture logs, photographs, invoices and emails make up your file.

Working With a Restoration Company During a Claim

A restoration company can start mitigation while you wait for the insurer. That work is meant to stop further damage, which is exactly what policies ask you to do. Ask for a written scope and for moisture logs.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and we keep records you can send to your adjuster. We do not decide coverage, and we do not argue with your insurer on your behalf.

Common Claim Mistakes

A few habits slow claims down.

Cleaning before photographing

Once wet materials are gone, the adjuster has less to look at. Photograph first, then remove.

Throwing away evidence of the source

Keep the failed part, such as a burst hose or a cracked fitting, if the plumber allows it. It may help show what happened.

Missing receipts

Emergency work, equipment and hotel stays are easier to reimburse with receipts. Photograph paper receipts as a backup.

Waiting too long to file

DFS says to file as soon as possible. Delays can complicate how the loss is judged.

If the Claim Is Large or Slow

Larger losses take longer, especially when several trades are involved. Keep a simple log of calls, emails and dates. Ask for each decision in writing, and ask what documents would help.

If you disagree with a decision, ask for the reason and the policy language it relies on. DFS accepts complaints from consumers who have concerns about how a claim is handled.

Working With the Adjuster

The adjuster is the insurer's representative. A calm, organized conversation helps.

Show the evidence

Walk the adjuster through photos, the source of the leak and the readings taken by the restoration crew.

Ask what happens next

Ask when you will hear about coverage and what documents are still needed.

Keep your own notes

Write down what was said and by whom. Follow up with a short email so there is a written trail.

Do not guess about cause

If you are unsure why a pipe failed, say so. Let the plumber and the adjuster work it out.

Contents, Structure and Living Expenses

A claim usually has several parts: the building, your belongings and sometimes extra living costs if you cannot stay in the home. Each part has its own documentation. Photographs of damaged belongings and a list with age and value help the contents part. Hotel and meal receipts help the living expenses part, if your policy includes it.

Check each part of your policy so you know what to ask for.

A Sample Claim Timeline

Day of the loss: stop the water, photograph, call the insurer and arrange emergency mitigation. Within a few days: adjuster inspects, restoration crew continues drying and you collect receipts. Within weeks: the insurer reviews the claim, issues a decision and payment.

Repairs follow once the scope is agreed. Timing varies a lot, so ask your adjuster for realistic expectations.

A Note on Mold and Claims

If water sits long enough for mold to appear, the claim can become harder. Prompt drying reduces that risk and shows the insurer you acted quickly. Keep the drying logs, since they show when the structure was dry.

Local Notes for Brentwood Claims

Many Brentwood homes have finished basements, which are the most common place for water losses. If the water came from outside, DFS notes a typical homeowners policy does not cover flood, and a flood policy or back-up endorsement may apply.

For permits on larger repairs, contact the Town of Islip Building Division. If your home is in nearby Islip Terrace, the same Town of Islip rules apply.
