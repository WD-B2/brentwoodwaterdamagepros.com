Water Damage Restoration Estimate Checklist for Brentwood, NY

A good estimate lists the scope room by room, the equipment and drying days, what is removed, what is rebuilt and what is not included. If a quote is a single number with no detail, ask for the breakdown before you agree.

By the Brentwood Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026

Why the Estimate Matters

Callers usually ask for a quote first. A clear estimate protects both sides. It lets you compare companies, and it gives your insurer something specific to review.

We explain scope and price before work starts. This guide shows what to look for in any estimate, ours or someone else's.

What a Water Damage Estimate Should Include

SectionWhat it should sayRed flag if missing
Scope by room.Rooms, areas and materials affected.Only a total square footage.
Water category.Clean, gray or black water.No mention of water type.
Extraction.Method and hours.No details.
Drying equipment.Number of air movers and dehumidifiers and expected days.Only a flat equipment fee.
Demolition.Materials to be removed.Vague tear-out line.
Rebuild.Drywall, paint, flooring if included.Unclear whether rebuild is included.
Exclusions.What is not covered.No exclusions listed.

Questions to Ask

Ask these before agreeing.

How was the scope measured?

Look for moisture readings and a walk-through, not a guess over the phone.

What changes the price?

More drying days or hidden damage found after opening walls can change it. Ask how changes are handled.

Who pays for what?

Ask what part is mitigation and what part is repair, since insurers may treat them differently.

Is there a written work order?

Get it in writing before work begins.

Mitigation Versus Repair

Mitigation stops further damage: extraction, removal of wet materials and drying. Repair rebuilds what was removed. They are often separate lines and sometimes separate companies.

Mitigation is usually time-sensitive, so it may start before the repair scope is final. See water extraction and structural drying for how those steps work.

Inspection Language to Watch For

You may see offers of free work. Be careful and read the terms. An inspection or assessment explains what is wet and what the work involves. It is part of the process, not a reason to skip a written scope.

A reliable company shows moisture readings during the inspection and explains how they affect the plan.

Estimates and Insurance

If you are filing a claim, the estimate matters twice.

Mitigation estimate

This explains what is needed to stop damage now.

Repair estimate

This describes the rebuild. Your insurer may have its own estimate and may adjust.

Keep both

Keep every version with dates, since NY DFS advises keeping records of all losses and repairs.

Why Estimates Differ Between Companies

Two companies can look at the same loss and write different numbers for good reasons.

Different scopes

One may remove more drywall and another may try to dry it in place. Ask each why.

Different equipment plans

Equipment counts depend on room volume and humidity. More units for fewer days can cost the same as fewer units for more days.

Different assumptions

One quote may include rebuild while another covers mitigation only.

Different standards

Ask whether the plan follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 drying approach and how completion will be measured.

Signing Safely in an Emergency

When water is running, you may need to sign a work authorization quickly. Read it. Make sure it describes mitigation work, allows you to approve additional work in writing and states how payment works.

You can authorize emergency steps and still ask for the full scope and price in writing before bigger decisions. A company that explains the numbers before starting is easier to work with than one that explains them after.

What Happens if Hidden Damage Appears

Opening a wall sometimes reveals more.

Stop and show you

A good company stops, photographs the finding and shows you.

Update the scope

The change is written down with a reason and a price.

Approve in writing

You approve additional work before it begins.

Timing and Payment Terms

Ask when payment is due and what triggers it. Insurance payments often come in stages. Some companies work directly with insurers and some ask homeowners to pay and seek reimbursement. Clarify this before work starts.

Do not pay the full amount before work begins. Ask what happens if the job takes longer or shorter than planned.

Bring These to the Estimate Visit

  • Your insurance policy information.
  • Photos you already took.
  • A list of rooms that were wet.
  • Details on the source and when it was fixed.

Estimates for Different Kinds of Loss

A clean water pipe burst is usually simpler to estimate than a sewage backup or a flood. Contaminated water adds protective steps and more removal. A crawl space is harder to access than an open floor. A commercial space may need work in off hours.

Ask the company how the type of loss shapes the scope. A good answer connects the type of water, the materials and the equipment plan.

After You Receive an Estimate

Read it twice. Mark every line you do not understand and ask about each. Compare it with the readings and photos you saw during the visit. If you plan to file a claim, send a copy to your adjuster and ask whether they need changes.

Be Careful With Verbal Promises

A phone price is not an estimate. If someone promises a total over the phone, ask them to put it in writing with the scope. A written document is what you can compare, and it protects you if the work changes.

Estimates and Your Own Records

Keep your own file next to the estimate. Include photos, notes from the walk-through, the dates of each call and the names of the people you spoke with. If the final invoice differs from the estimate, your file explains why and helps you ask the right questions.

Local Notes for Brentwood

Brentwood homes often have finished basements, where scope can change once drywall is opened. Ask how a company handles that. If a permit is needed for rebuild work, contact the Town of Islip Building Division.
