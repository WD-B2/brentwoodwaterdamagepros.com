Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Brentwood, NY?

Sometimes. Homeowners policies often cover sudden, accidental water damage such as a burst pipe, but they typically exclude flood and gradual leaks. New York DFS says a typical homeowners policy does not cover flood. Your own policy decides, so read it and ask your agent.

By the Brentwood Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026

The Short Answer by Cause

Coverage depends on what caused the water. Insurers separate sudden, accidental losses from slow, long-term ones, and they separate water that comes from inside the house from water that comes from outside. Two homes with the same wet floor can get different answers.

This guide is general information, not insurance advice. Your policy language is what counts.

Flood, Back-Up and Why They Matter Here

Two terms trip people up more than any others.

Flood

DFS states that the typical homeowners policy does not cover flood damage. Coverage can come from a flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program or from a private flood policy. Flood has a specific meaning in policies, so ask what counts.

Water back-up

DFS notes that a policy may cover back-up and sewage damage through an endorsement, but typically excludes it when the cause is flood. If you have a finished basement, ask whether you have this endorsement.

What Insurers Ask For After a Loss

Insurers usually want proof of what happened and what it cost. Photographs and video taken before cleanup, moisture readings, receipts for emergency work and a list of damaged items all help.

DFS recommends documenting losses before cleaning up, filing the claim as soon as possible and limiting early work to what is needed for health, safety or to prevent more damage. It also says permanent repairs should wait until the insurer has inspected the loss.

Questions to Ask Your Agent

Ask these before you need to file.

What counts as sudden?

Ask how the policy defines sudden and accidental, and how it treats a leak that was found after it had been running for a while.

Do I have a back-up endorsement?

If your basement has a floor drain or a sump pump, this matters.

What is my deductible for water?

Some policies set a separate deductible for certain water or storm losses.

Does my policy cover mold?

Mold coverage varies and may be limited. See mold remediation for how cleanup works.

Common Policy Words That Change the Answer

Policies use specific terms. Reading them closely often explains a denial or an approval.

Sudden and accidental

Most coverage hinges on this phrase. A pipe that bursts overnight fits. A fitting that has dripped for months usually does not.

Continuous or repeated seepage

Many policies exclude damage from water that leaked over a long period. If the loss was found late, an insurer may argue it was gradual.

Wear and tear and maintenance

Policies often exclude the cost of repairing the failed part itself, such as the old pipe, while still covering resulting water damage. Ask how yours splits the two.

Limits and deductibles

Some policies cap certain water losses or use a separate deductible. Knowing the numbers before a loss avoids surprises.

Before You Call Your Insurer

Have the basics ready: the date the water was found, what you think caused it, which rooms are affected and what steps you have taken. Photographs and a rough list of damaged items speed up the first call.

Ask the adjuster what they need from you, and keep notes with names and dates. If emergency drying is needed, tell them you are starting it to prevent more damage. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to protect the property.

Documents to Gather Before You File

  • Your policy declarations page and any endorsements.
  • Photos or video of each affected room before cleanup.
  • A list of damaged belongings with approximate values.
  • Invoices for the plumber, roofer or restoration company.
  • The adjuster's name, claim number and dates of each conversation.

If Coverage Is Denied or Partly Paid

Ask for the denial in writing with the policy language cited. Compare it with the policy yourself and with what your agent tells you. If you believe the decision is wrong, ask for a review. DFS takes consumer complaints about insurers and has published guidance on disaster and flood claims.
