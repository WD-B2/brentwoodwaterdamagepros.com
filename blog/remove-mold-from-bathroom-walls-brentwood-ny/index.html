How to Remove Mold From Bathroom Walls in Brentwood, NY

Small spots on hard bathroom surfaces can often be cleaned with detergent and water after the moisture source is fixed. Mold on drywall, growth over a large area or a persistent smell is a job for a professional. In New York, projects over 10 square feet involve licensed assessors and remediators.

By the Brentwood Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026

Start With the Moisture

Mold grows where a bathroom stays wet. A shower leak, a toilet leak, poor ventilation or a pipe inside the wall can all be the cause. Cleaning without fixing the moisture is a short-lived fix.

Callers often describe mold at the corner of a shower wall and ceiling, or a bathroom where spots keep returning. That pattern points to ongoing moisture.

What You Can Reasonably Do Yourself

For a small area on a hard, non-porous surface, these steps are standard.

Protect yourself

Wear gloves, eye protection and a dust mask. Open a window or run the exhaust fan.

Clean with detergent and water

Scrub the surface with detergent and water and dry it completely. Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners because the fumes are dangerous.

Dry and ventilate

Run the fan during and after showers and dry wet surfaces. Fix leaks quickly.

Why Drywall Is Different

Mold on tile can be wiped away. Mold on drywall grows into the paper and gypsum, so surface cleaning does not remove it. Drywall with growth is usually cut out and replaced after the moisture is fixed and the cavity is dried.

That is why we start with drying and then mold remediation when needed.

What New York Requires

New York regulates mold work through the State Department of Labor.

The size threshold

The DOL describes a mold project as work on areas greater than ten square feet. Homeowners working on their own residence are exempt.

Separate assessor and remediator

No licensee may do both assessment and remediation on the same project, according to the DOL.

Ask for the license

If you hire a contractor for a mold project, ask to see the license.

Health Questions

We do not make health claims about mold. If someone in the house has symptoms, talk to a physician.

Why Bathrooms Grow Mold

A bathroom is built to get wet and dry out. Mold appears when the drying part fails.

Poor ventilation

An exhaust fan that is too weak or that vents into the attic leaves steam in the room.

Hidden leaks

A shower valve, a tub surround seam or a supply line can leak inside the wall and feed growth from behind.

Grout and caulk failure

Cracked caulk lets water behind tile. Replacing it is cheap compared to opening a wall.

Keeping It From Coming Back

Run the fan during every shower and for at least twenty minutes after. Wipe down wet surfaces. Re-caulk failing seams and fix drips quickly. A bathroom without a window needs a fan that actually moves air, so check by holding tissue near it.

If spots return in the same place, there is probably moisture behind that spot. Stop scrubbing and have it checked.

Rented and Shared Bathrooms

Who handles mold depends on who owns the space.

Tenants

Notify the landlord in writing and keep photographs. Ask a housing advocate about your rights.

Landlords

Fix the water source and dry the space. For larger areas, follow New York licensing rules.

Condo owners

Shared walls and plumbing may belong to the association. Check your documents.

A Simple Weekly Habit

Squeegee the shower walls, wipe the sink area and check under the vanity for drips. It takes a few minutes and catches problems early. Wash bath mats often and let them dry fully. If a shower curtain or tile is dark at the bottom edge, clean it right away before growth spreads.

Bathroom Surfaces and How They React

Tile and glazed surfaces do not absorb water, so growth stays on top and wipes away. Grout is porous and holds growth longer. Painted drywall and wood trim take in water and can hold growth inside.

Caulk is a common trouble spot. Black spots in caulk usually mean the caulk has aged and trapped moisture. Remove old caulk and replace it after the seam is dry. If the wall behind it is soft, the problem is bigger than the caulk.

When the Ceiling Is Involved

Bathroom ceilings often show mold from steam that condenses above the shower. Cleaning a painted ceiling surface can help if the drywall is firm and dry. If the ceiling stains again soon after, check for a leak above, a failing exhaust duct or an attic that is too humid.

See ceiling water damage repair if the ceiling is stained, soft or sagging.

Choosing Bathroom Materials After Repair

When a wall is rebuilt, consider moisture-resistant drywall in wet areas, a sealed shower surround and a quality exhaust fan sized for the room. Use caulk made for wet areas and replace it as it ages.

These choices do not make mold impossible, but they give the room a better chance of staying dry between uses.

Bathrooms in Brentwood Homes

Many Brentwood homes have older bathrooms with original plumbing and limited ventilation. Humid summers make it harder to dry a bathroom naturally.
