Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Brentwood, NY?

Drying and cleanup usually do not need a permit, while structural, electrical and plumbing repairs often do. Brentwood is in the Town of Islip, whose Building Division handles building and plumbing permits. Ask the Division before work, since rules depend on the job.

By the Brentwood Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026

Permits and Water Damage: The Short Version

People often ask whether they need a permit before fixing water damage. It depends on what the work involves. Cleaning and drying are different from opening walls, moving plumbing or changing electrical wiring.

This guide is general. The Building Division decides what applies to your job.

Types of Work and Typical Permit Questions

These are patterns. Confirm with the Building Division.

Who Handles It in Brentwood

Brentwood is a hamlet in the Town of Islip, Suffolk County.

Town of Islip Building Division

The Town of Islip says the Building Division administers building and plumbing permit applications, reviewed for compliance with the New York State Uniform Code, the Town Zoning Code and the Subdivision and Land Development Regulations.

How to apply

The Division asks that applications be submitted electronically. Check its page for the current process.

Inspections

Inspections are scheduled through the Records Office, not by inspectors, according to the Town.

Who Pulls the Permit

The contractor doing the permitted work usually applies, and the homeowner is responsible for the property. Ask who will apply before you sign. Unpermitted work can cause problems when you sell or file an insurance claim.

We handle drying and restoration. A licensed plumber or electrician handles those trades, and permits for them follow their scope.

Insurers sometimes ask for permit records on larger repairs.

Keep permit documents with your claim file.

Passing inspection is part of a documented repair.

What to Ask the Building Division

A short call or email avoids guesswork.

Describe the work

Say what will be removed, replaced or moved, and whether plumbing, wiring or framing is involved.

Ask about the property

Give the address and ask whether any flood or zoning rules affect the work.

Ask about timing

Ask how long review takes so you can plan around drying and rebuild schedules.

Ask about inspections

Ask when inspections are required and how they are scheduled.

Why Skipping a Permit Can Cost You Later

Unpermitted work can surface when you sell, refinance or file an insurance claim. A buyer's inspector may ask for records. A lender may care. An insurer may question repairs that cannot be shown as compliant.

Permit fees and inspections add some time and cost, but they leave a clear record. That record is worth having if a leak happens again and the repair is questioned.

Keep These in One Folder

  • Permit application and approval.
  • Inspection sign-offs.
  • Contractor names and licenses.
  • Photos of the work before walls were closed.

Permit Situations People Overlook

A few repairs trigger permits more often than expected.

Water heater replacement

Swapping a failed tank often involves plumbing, venting and sometimes gas connections.

Basement finishing

Rebuilding a finished basement may involve egress, electrical and insulation requirements.

Moving or adding walls

Changing layout is different from patching what was there.

Structural repair

Replacing joists or subfloor sections can involve engineering questions.

Working With Contractors on Permits

Ask each contractor whether the job needs a permit and who will pull it. Get that in writing. A contractor who says permits are unnecessary for plumbing or electrical replacement is worth a second question to the Building Division.

Licensed trades usually know their local requirements. A restoration crew handles drying and finish repairs, and the other trades handle theirs.

Permits for Larger Water Damage Rebuilds

After a major loss, a rebuild may involve new framing, new wiring, new plumbing runs and finishes. Each of these can bring its own permit requirements and inspections. Plan for them in the schedule.

A contractor can pull permits for their own trade. For a full rebuild, many homeowners hire a general contractor who coordinates permits and inspections for all trades.

Permits and Contractors You Hire

Ask every contractor for their license or registration where the trade requires one and keep a copy. A good contractor will explain which parts of the work need permits and who pulls them. If they avoid the question, ask the Building Division yourself.

Emergency Work and Permits

In an emergency, crews stop the damage first. Extraction, removal of wet materials and drying are mitigation steps and happen right away. Permit questions usually arise once rebuild work is planned.

If you are unsure whether emergency steps affect a later permit, ask the Building Division and keep notes of what was done and when.

Permit Records and Your Home's History

A permit record stays with the property. Future buyers, lenders and insurers can see that work was inspected. Keep your copies, since municipal files are not always easy to retrieve years later.

If you inherit a home with past water repairs, ask the Building Division whether permits were issued for them.

Local Notes for Brentwood

Brentwood properties are under the Town of Islip, but nearby Hauppauge sits partly in the Town of Islip and partly in the Town of Smithtown. If you are near that area, ask which town handles your parcel.
