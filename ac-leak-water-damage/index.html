Why an AC Unit Leaks Inside the House

Air conditioners pull water out of the air and send it away through a condensate drain line. When that line clogs, the pan overflows and water goes where gravity sends it. A condensation line that has been backing up is a real cause we hear about from callers.

The result is a damp closet, a ceiling stain below an air handler or a wet spot near a wall unit. The damage is slow, which is why it is often found late.

Where AC Water Ends Up

Location depends on where the air handler is installed.

Attic air handlers

Water drips onto ceiling insulation and drywall below. A stain can appear on a bedroom ceiling long after the pan overflowed.

Closet and utility room units

Water runs across the closet floor and wicks into carpet and wall bottoms. A wet spot in a child's closet is a common first sign.

Basement and garage units

Water pools at the base of the unit and may run to a floor drain or into stored items.

AC Leaks and Mold

Slow condensate leaks feed mold quietly. If you find dark spots or a musty smell near the unit, see mold remediation. Fix the drain line first or the problem will return.

Stopping an AC Leak From Coming Back

Drying the damage solves today's problem. The drain line decides whether you face it again next summer.

Clear the condensate line

An HVAC technician can flush the line and check the pan. A line that carries algae and dust clogs more than once, so ask about a routine flush before cooling season.

Consider a safety switch

A float switch shuts the system off when the pan fills. It does not fix a clog, but it limits how much water ever reaches your ceiling.

Watch for early signs

A damp smell near the air handler, a rusty pan or a stain on the ceiling that returns after painting all point to a recurring leak.

What to Tell Us When You Call

Tell us where the air handler sits and where the water showed up. Say whether the system is running, since turning it off stops new water. Mention any stains you have noticed before, because a repeating stain tells us the leak has been slow and long.

We use that picture to bring the right tools and to decide whether insulation, carpet or drywall are likely in play.

What to Do and What Not to Do With an AC Leak

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Turn the air conditioner off at the thermostat and the breaker so it stops making more condensate.
  • Do not run the unit to see whether the leak returns, because that pushes more water into the ceiling.
  • Place a bucket under any drip and poke nothing into a sagging ceiling unless water is pooling above it.
  • Stay out of rooms where a wet ceiling bulges over a light fixture or an outlet.

Humid Summers and Condensate Lines

Brentwood summers run humid, and an AC works hard, so condensate volume is high. A line that is not flushed can clog and back up.

Ask an HVAC contractor to clear the line and set up a float switch where practical. If walls or ceilings are opened, ask your building department whether a permit applies.
