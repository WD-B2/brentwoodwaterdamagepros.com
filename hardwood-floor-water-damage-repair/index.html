Why Hardwood Cups After a Leak

Wood absorbs water through its underside and edges. The bottom of the board swells faster than the top, so the edges lift and the board cups. A washer valve that broke and ran for an hour, or an air conditioner condensate line that backed up, is enough to start it.

Cupping can appear a day or two after the leak, even when the surface looked fine at first. Callers describe walnut flooring that began to cup well after the water was gone.

Cupping, Crowning and Buckling

The shape tells you where in the process the floor is.

Cupping

Board edges are higher than the center. It is the first and most common response to moisture from below.

Crowning

The center is higher than the edges, often after a cupped floor is sanded before it dries.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor. This is serious and often means replacement of the affected section.

How We Dry Hardwood

Read moisture

Meters show how wet the boards and subfloor are.

Dry with control

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the floor, and drying panels or mats may be used for pulled boards.

Monitor

Readings are checked until boards reach their normal moisture level.

Decide

After drying, we sand and refinish or replace boards that did not recover.

Do Not Sand Too Soon

Sanding a floor that is still damp can lock in cupping and cause crowning. Wait until readings show the boards are dry, then refinish. Fast, controlled drying is cheaper than replacement, but it has to start early.

If the source was a leak, see appliance leak water damage or water extraction for the first steps.

Why Subfloor and Humidity Matter

Hardwood does not dry by itself from the top down.

The underside holds water

Boards are exposed to moisture from below, where air does not reach. That is why drying methods sometimes pull moisture from the underside, not just fan the surface.

Humidity in the room

High indoor humidity slows drying. Dehumidifiers keep the air dry enough for the boards to release water.

Finish is not a seal

Varnish slows water but does not stop it. Water enters through seams, edges and cracks in the finish.

Questions for a Flooring Pro After Drying

  • Are the boards back to a normal moisture reading?
  • Is any cupping likely to flatten without sanding?
  • Should a section be replaced to match the grain?
  • How long should the floor sit before refinishing?

Engineered Hardwood and Laminate

Engineered hardwood has a thin wood layer on a plywood or composite base, so it reacts differently from solid planks. Laminate is a photo layer on fiberboard and swells permanently when soaked, often beyond saving.

We identify the product during assessment and tell you plainly if a floor type is unlikely to recover.

What to Do and What Not to Do With a Wet Hardwood Floor

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Remove standing water and lift rugs and furniture so air reaches the boards.
  • Do not sand, wax or seal a floor that has not finished drying, since that traps moisture below.
  • Do not run a space heater at the floor, which can crack boards as they dry unevenly.
  • Wait for a moisture meter reading before deciding to replace boards. Cupping often eases as wood dries.
  • Drying goals for wood follow ANSI/IICRC S500 and are checked against a dry reference reading.

Hardwood in Long Island Homes

Many Brentwood homes have hardwood floors over a basement or crawl space, where humidity can rise under the boards. Humid summers also affect moisture content.

Ask your building department about permits if subfloor or structure is rebuilt.
