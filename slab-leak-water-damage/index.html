Hot Spots, Wet Floors or a Jumping Water Bill?

A floor that feels warm in one spot, a damp patch on tile, the sound of running water when everything is off or a water bill that suddenly jumps can all point to a leak below the floor. People rarely search for the term slab leak first. They search for the symptom.

That symptom has a name in the trade: a slab leak, meaning a pressurized line that has cracked or corroded under the concrete. Finding it is the plumber's job. Drying the structure and the floor it wet is ours.

Slab Leak Emergency Signs

These are the signs that should make you call, and in some cases shut off the main.

Sounds and pressure

A hissing or running sound with every fixture off, or a pressure drop across the house, suggests water is escaping underground.

Floor symptoms

Warm patches on tile, buckled laminate, curling vinyl or damp carpet along a wall can mean water is rising from below.

Meter behavior

Close all fixtures and check the water meter. If it keeps moving, you have a leak somewhere between the meter and the faucets.

Why Drying Matters After a Slab Leak

Water under a slab rises into flooring, baseboards and the bottom of walls. Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so drying is slower than on a wood floor. Without drying, you can end up with mold growth along baseboards and a musty smell that does not go away.

We use moisture meters on flooring and wall bottoms and set air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the space. For deeper moisture in framing and slabs, see structural drying.

Insurance and Slab Leaks

Coverage for slab leaks depends on the policy, the cause and whether damage was sudden. Some policies cover the resulting water damage but not the pipe repair. Read your policy or ask your agent, and document the damage as DFS recommends.

Checks You Can Do Before the Plumber Arrives

  • Turn off all fixtures and see if the water meter still moves.
  • Walk barefoot across tile to feel for warm spots.
  • Listen near walls and floors for running water.
  • Compare the last few water bills for an unexplained rise.

What to Do and What Not to Do With a Suspected Slab Leak

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Turn off the main valve and watch the meter. If it keeps moving, the leak is still active.
  • Do not ignore warm floor spots or a hissing sound under the floor.
  • Do not drill or break the slab yourself, since the lines or wiring below may be live.
  • Call a licensed plumber to locate the line, then call us to dry the floor and walls.
  • Drying after a slab leak follows ANSI/IICRC S500 and tracks readings until the slab and walls reach their goal.

Slab Foundations and Suffolk County Homes

Many Long Island homes sit on slabs or have slab additions, and water lines under them are not visible. A water bill that rises with no change in use is worth checking against your utility bill.

If a slab is opened, ask your building department whether a permit applies. Brentwood properties under the Town of Islip go through its Building Division.
