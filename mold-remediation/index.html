Seeing Dark Spots or Smelling Mustiness?

Dark spots at a corner of the wall and ceiling, a musty smell near a closet or growth under carpet you pulled up all point to moisture that did not get dried. Callers describe mold in a bathroom, under a sink after a leak, in a closet near an AC unit and in a finished basement ceiling after an upstairs toilet leak.

Cleaning up that growth is called mold remediation. It starts with the water, not the mold.

Fix the Water First

Mold needs moisture. If the source is still active, cleanup will not last.

Common sources

Plumbing leaks, toilet and sink supply lines, AC condensate lines, roof leaks and siding leaks are the usual causes. See AC leak water damage and ceiling water damage repair.

Dry the structure

Materials must reach dry standards before and after cleanup. See structural drying for how that works.

Then remediate

Once the source is fixed and the area is dry, growth can be removed and the area cleaned.

How New York Splits Mold Work

New York requires licensed professionals for mold projects over a size threshold. The rules separate the assessor from the remediator.

RoleWhat they doNotes
Licensed mold assessor.Inspects, tests and writes a remediation plan.Cannot also remediate the same project.
Licensed mold remediator.Carries out the plan and removes growth.Cannot also assess the same project.
Homeowner.May work on own residential property.Exempt from license rules.
Clearance.Independent assessor confirms the result.Often needed for loans and sales.

Source: New York State Department of Labor mold program. Ask any contractor to show a current license.

A Mold Cleanup Sequence

Assess and plan

An independent licensed assessor tests and writes the plan when the job requires one.

Contain

The work area is sealed and air is filtered to limit spread.

Remove and clean

Growth and affected materials are removed and surfaces cleaned.

Verify

Clearance testing confirms the area is ready to rebuild.

Mold Testing, Inspection and Clearance

Callers ask about testing, assessment and clearance before sales or loans.

Testing and inspection

An assessor looks at moisture, visible growth and sources. Testing helps define the scope.

Clearance for a home sale or loan

Buyers sometimes find that a lender will not fund until mold is removed. Independent clearance testing documents the result. We make no promises about what a lender will accept.

Salvageability

After a leak, people ask what can be saved. Moisture readings and visual checks decide that.

Health Questions and Insurance

We are not medical professionals and make no health claims about exposure. Talk to a physician about symptoms.

Insurance coverage for mold depends on the policy and the cause. Check your policy and ask your agent. DFS advises documenting the loss and filing promptly.

What Mold Remediation Involves, Step by Step

Remediation is a controlled process designed to remove growth without spreading it through the house.

Containment

Plastic sheeting seals the work area. Negative air machines with HEPA filters pull air out through filtration so spores do not drift into clean rooms.

Removal

Porous materials with growth, such as drywall and insulation, are cut out and bagged. Non-porous and structural surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuums and damp wiping.

Drying and rebuilding

Everything must read dry before new materials go in. Rebuilding with damp framing starts the cycle again.

Questions to Ask Any Mold Contractor

A clear plan and a license check protect you.

Ask for the license

In New York, mold remediation above the threshold must be done by a licensed remediator. Ask to see it and compare the name and number with the state record.

Ask for the written plan

The assessor writes the remediation plan. The remediator writes a work plan that fulfills it and gives it to you before work begins.

Ask about clearance

An independent assessor, not the remediator, confirms the area passed. This is the document a lender or buyer may ask for.

What to Do and What Not to Do When You Find Mold

Follow these steps while you wait for the crew.

  • Fix or stop the water source first, because mold returns where moisture remains.
  • Do not scrub or sand moldy material, which spreads spores through the house.
  • Do not run the HVAC fan while mold is visible near a vent or air handler.
  • Wear an N95 mask and gloves for any short task near visible growth, and keep children and anyone with breathing problems out. The S520 guide is the industry reference for mold remediation.

Mold and Humid Long Island Summers

Suffolk County summers are humid, and basements and closets hold moisture. A leak plus humidity is enough for growth to start.

In New York, the mold program is run by the State Department of Labor. Ask your building department about permits for remediation or rebuild work.
